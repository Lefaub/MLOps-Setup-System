# Prospective assisted-versus-manual comparison — not executed

**Question.** With identical requirements and starting infrastructure, how much engineer time is needed to reach the same usable deployment, and what happens when requirements change? The survey and CI timings do not answer this question.

**Design.** Recruit practitioners beyond the original networks and record relevant Kubernetes/MLOps experience. Before recruitment, specify a minimally important time difference, use independent pilot variability to justify the sample size, and predefine outcomes and time limits. Use two comparable tasks and balanced assignment of method and task order; no participant repeats the identical task with both methods. Give both arms the same pinned components, documentation, permissions and clean cluster state, including image-cache conditions. The manual arm configures without the product-line generator. Use equivalent resources and training in each arm.

**Outcome and records.** Start the clock when the task and ready environment are handed over; stop when the independent acceptance suite first confirms the specified services, ready endpoints and a successful application round-trip. Record elapsed and active engineer time separately, configuration attempts, invalid combinations, deployment failures, unassisted recoveries, external help and completion at the time limit. Record failures and incomplete tasks rather than dropping them from the results. A subsequent change to one service endpoint or one required capability can test maintenance time, errors and repeatability; longer-term maintenance would require a longitudinal study.

**Analysis.** Compare completion as well as time; report uncertainty and define handling of timeouts in advance. Account for participants, task and order in the analysis. Keep benchmark fixtures separate from evaluation of model accuracy and explanation quality. No observations from this proposed comparison are reported in the article.
